Terms on this page

Survey
The agent's reading of the repository, done in passes, with each conclusion tied to the code it read.
Subsystem
A part of the codebase the survey treats as one unit, with its own files and its own page. The agent chose the split during onboarding.
Layer
A group of subsystems with a similar role. Pages group subsystems by layer.
Survey depth
How far the survey of a subsystem has got. Unmapped: not started. Scoped and Structural: that pass is done. Reviewing concerns and Challenging claims: that pass is under way. Mapped: every pass is done. Deferred: left out of the survey on purpose. Each depth limits what the record may claim.
Mapped
The last survey depth: every pass has run. It does not mean every file is read or every bug found.
Scope
The files a subsystem owns, each marked as read, not yet read, or left out with a reason.
Evidence
A pointer to code at a commit (file, symbol, lines) with a note of how strong it is: read in code, seen in a test, stated in a comment, and so on.
Claim
A statement about how the code is built ("this cache is per request"), with evidence.
Challenge
A pass in which the agent tries to prove its own claims and findings wrong. Each claim records whether it survived.
Concern
One kind of defect worth looking for in this codebase, such as a race on a shared cache. The review checklist is the full list.
Concern review
The verdict for one concern in one subsystem: bug found, acceptable, ruled out, out of scope, or undecided.
Finding
A defect the survey confirmed, with evidence, a severity, and a state (open, repaired, verified, ruled out, accepted).
Lead
Something the agent noticed that is not a finding: a pattern, an oddity, a tension, a possible concern.
Decision needed
Something the agent could not settle alone. It records the assumption it used and keeps going; you answer it.
System boundary
A place where two subsystems share something (a table, a queue, a cache) and depend on each other's assumptions about it.
Recorded edge
A dependency or data flow between two subsystems that the agent wrote down. Edges are never guessed from names.
Standing
What the record says about one file: no record, left out, in scope but unread, read, read but changed since.
Purpose, account
A subsystem's one-sentence purpose and its longer explanation of how it works, each written from evidence.

Reader's guide

Golden corpus generator and rebuildable goldens

§Purpose

The golden corpus records the materializer's HTML page by page. The store-only tests rebuild each store fixture from its memory.sql and check today's pages match the recorded ones byte for byte, bar two workspace-bound lens pages. make-golden.py writes the corpus from an in-process materializer run, keeping what the HTML stage was given and what it printed. It writes any --packet name to VERSION.

Recorded at 94d98773.

§Scope

materializer/make-golden.py and the store-only golden corpus it writes (assets/, axiomdb-shaped/, materializer/, materializer-github/, self-shaped/, VERSION), plus the preserved hooks/ pages it cannot rebuild.

Start here materializer/make-golden.py

§Survey note

240 ledger rows, of which make-golden.py and the 9 hooks/ files must be read; the 230 rows under the rebuildable directories may be generated-ignore (lane rule D1) once the generator is read and shown to rewrite them. make-golden.py's docstring says the md/ pages were retired by SO19, which a surveyor should check before marking them generated.

§How it works

make-golden.py writes the corpus that the store-only tests compare the materializer's HTML against, byte for byte. The corpus is committed, so a renderer change that alters a compared page must regenerate it in the same commit.

capture_publish runs materialize.py in process with core.render_html_projection wrapped, and keeps what that stage was given (page plan, context, locus index, notice and page models) and the HTML it wrote. write_fixture saves everything but the models as inputs.json, the pages under html/ and, for a store fixture, a dump of the store as memory.sql. It then reprints the HTML from the recorded inputs and the in-memory models and exits if any page differs. fold replaces each bare <style> or <script> body of 1,024 characters or more with @@golden-asset <sha256>@@ and stores the body once under assets/. It refuses a page that does not unfold to its own bytes.

The materializer fixture is the store test-materializer.py seeds. materializer-github reprints its inputs with a GitHub URL and the ref main to pin source links, and has no store of its own. axiomdb-shaped and self-shaped are committed legacy dumps that the freshly built server migrates and imports through node.

A full run deletes the directory except hooks/ and rebuilds the other five fixtures. --recorded loads each store's memory.sql into a scratch store and publishes it again. It overwrites pages already in the corpus, may add about.html, skips the two lens pages that depend on a git workspace, and refuses any other new page. hooks/ was printed through a Markdown parser that SO19 removed. No run can rebuild it.

Recorded at 94d98773.

§Concurrency invariants

  • make-golden.py is a single-process script that runs its fixtures one after another and takes no lock on the golden directory. Two overlapping full regenerations could each delete the directory while the other is writing. The committed fixtures have no runtime state.
    • M-07/concurrency · subject M-07 · Observation · asserted at ec393801fd81 · evidence: materializer/make-golden.py:396-406

§Structure

The corpus is the committed expected output of the HTML stage, written by one script, make-golden.py. Each fixture directory holds inputs.json (the page plan, shell context, locus index and notice that render_html_projection received), its html/ pages and, for a store fixture, a memory.sql dump of the store the pages came from. materializer-github has no store of its own: it reprints the materializer fixture's captured inputs and models with a GitHub URL and the branch main, to pin GitHub source links.

capture_publish runs the materializer CLI in process with core.render_html_projection wrapped, and keeps the first call's arguments, the page models and the HTML written. write_fixture writes the inputs, HTML and any store dump, then prints the HTML again from those inputs and the in-memory models and exits if any page differs. That catches a page that changes between renders, such as one reading a clock or following file order. A long bare <style> or <script> body is folded into assets/ under its SHA-256 and unfolded before any comparison. Pages now link shared site assets, so only the hooks pages and lens not-yet-surveyed.html still hold folds.

The materializer fixture comes from test-materializer.py's seed plus imported onboarding prose, and lens from test-lens-pages.py's own workspace and seed. axiomdb-shaped and self-shaped come from committed legacy dumps that the server, rebuilt with npm, migrates and imports.

hooks/ holds hand-written Markdown pages and the HTML printed from them, and no run rebuilds it. --recorded keeps the stores and prints the four store fixtures' HTML again from memory.sql. It skips the two workspace-dependent lens pages and refuses a page the corpus lacks, about.html excepted. Both runs write the packet name to VERSION.

§Vocabulary

  • golden corpus — The committed store-only fixture set that pins the materializer's HTML byte for byte. For each fixture it holds the HTML stage's inputs, the folded HTML pages, and usually the store they were printed from.
  • recognition hooks fixture — The golden fixture "hooks": written Markdown pages, with their golden HTML, that reach parser hooks no store fixture prints. These include Mermaid graphs, nested lists, quotes, fences and misnested emphasis.

§M07-1

Claim B, what would overturn it: a writer or reader of md/ somewhere else, or proof that the trees still match what the pages say.

  • No writer: write_fixture writes inputs.json, html/ and memory.sql only (materializer/make-golden.py:write_fixture@ec393801, evidence 2843), and --recorded writes html/ pages only (make-golden.py:reprint_recorded@ec393801, evidence 2856).
  • No reader: the only md/ the tests open is the hooks fixture's, parsed by C2b of materializer/test-store-only-model.py (evidence 3057); the store fixtures are compared from html/ and memory.sql (evidence 2906).
  • The trees are stale, not merely unpinned. Every store fixture's md/how-to-read.md still defines excluded as only a ledger classification (self-shaped/md/how-to-read.md line 406, evidence 3049), while the html golden carries the survey-scope meaning (evidence 3051). The readers also found renamed page titles (Seams against System boundaries, Glossary against Codebase glossary) and html sections the md lacks.
  • materializer/md and materializer-github/md are byte-identical, though the html twins differ in entry-point.html's GitHub source link.

Verdict: upheld, quality-upgraded. The symptom now rests on a read of every md file, not on the generator alone. Severity stays LOW: nothing consumes these files, so the harm is a misleading corpus and ledger weight.
